We mentor students toward real physics and science competitions.

Unlike a knowledge-test format, physics competitions at the elementary level are mostly project- and team-based. Here's the real pathway we prepare students for — starting local, with a track record already established at Horace Mann.

Washington State Science and Engineering Fair (WSSEF)

WSSEF is open to students in grades 1–12 — not just high schoolers. Students design and carry out an original research project, then present it to judges. Our region qualifies through the Central Sound Regional Science and Engineering Fair (CSRSEF), serving King and Snohomish counties.

Highlights

  • Real, individual research projects — students pick a genuine testable question, not a worksheet.
  • A documented Horace Mann precedent: a first-grade student has won first prize and special awards at WSSEF.
  • Clear advancement pathway: school fair → CSRSEF → WSSEF → (for top projects) ISEF.
  • We mentor project selection, experimental design, and presentation — not just the science content.

Science Olympiad — Division A

Science Olympiad's Division A is built specifically for elementary school (grades K–6), distinct from the middle and high school divisions. Teams compete across events spanning physics, earth science, and life science.

Highlights

  • Team-based format — students work together across a shared slate of events.
  • We focus on the knowledge and lab-based events, matching our strength in hands-on scientific reasoning.
  • Division A "emphasizes meeting event standards rather than competition" — a lower-pressure entry point than the fair pathway.
  • A natural on-ramp to Division B in middle school for students who want to keep going.

ExploraVision

A K–12 science innovation competition where student teams envision and describe a future technology that solves a real problem. A written and illustrated format — no building or construction required.

Highlights

  • Rewards conceptual and scientific thinking over engineering/construction skills.
  • Team-based, open to elementary-aged students.
  • A good complement for students who enjoy the ideas behind physics more than hands-on building.
  • National recognition for top entries.
