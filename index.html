👋 Meet the Team

Arjun Nair, Redmond High School

Arjun Nair, a Presidential Service Award and Congressional Award Bronze Medal winner, founded Wavelength STEM after two years of teaching hands-on science and math at Horace Mann Elementary. His work in youth STEM education and community service — including organizing annual book drives across multiple LWSD schools that brought roughly 5,000 books to underpriviledged children — was recognized by the Lake Washington School District. He is a board member of the Redmond High School Physics club, has conducted independent and mentored research in waves and frequencies following his passion in this field. Wavelength STEM is the next chapter of that same commitment: bringing real, hands-on science to more elementary students across Redmond and ignite their curiosity at a very young age. Arjun also loves doing Taekwondo and playing Viola.

Aadya Sharma

Aadya Sharma is an innovation-minded high school student passionate about STEM, leadership, and community service. With nearly four years of experience volunteering across multiple school communities, she has participated in a variety of competitive STEM events and currently teaches classes to support students at Redmond Middle School as they prepare for Science Olympiad competitions. Through her work at Wavelength STEM, Aadya hopes to create a positive and engaging learning experience for elementary-aged students while inspiring a lifelong love for science. In her free time, she enjoys reading and exploring new places!

Is this a real, credible program?
Yes — it's led by Redmond High School students with multiple years of hands-on classroom teaching experience at Horace Mann Elementary and Redmond Middle School.
What will my child actually learn?
Hands-on science, how scientists collect real data, and — for kids who want to go further — how to design and present their own science fair project, following Washington's state science fair pathway.

No. This program does not charge fees and does not solicit donations of any kind.
How big are the groups?
Small and hands-on — every session is designed so kids actually get to touch, build, and try things themselves, not just watch.

🏆 We mentor students toward real physics and science competitions.

Unlike a knowledge-test format, science competitions at the elementary level are mostly project- and team-based. Here's the real pathway we prepare students for — starting local, with a track record already established at Horace Mann.

Mann Elementary PTSA Fair
→ CSRSEF (Regional)
→ WSSEF (State)
→ ISEF (International)

Washington State Science and Engineering Fair (WSSEF)

WSSEF is open to students in grades 1–12 — not just high schoolers. Students design and carry out an original research project, then present it to judges. Our region qualifies through the Central Sound Regional Science and Engineering Fair (CSRSEF), serving King and Snohomish counties.

Highlights

  • Real, individual research projects — students pick a genuine testable question, not a worksheet.
  • A documented Horace Mann precedent: a first-grade student has won first prize and special awards at WSSEF.
  • Clear advancement pathway: school fair → CSRSEF → WSSEF → (for top projects) ISEF.
  • We mentor project selection, experimental design, and presentation — not just the science content.

Science Olympiad — Division A

Science Olympiad's Division A is built specifically for elementary school (grades K–6), distinct from the middle and high school divisions. Teams compete across events spanning physics, earth science, and life science.

Highlights

  • Team-based format — students work together across a shared slate of events.
  • We focus on the knowledge and lab-based events, matching our strength in hands-on scientific reasoning.
  • Division A "emphasizes meeting event standards rather than competition" — a lower-pressure entry point than the fair pathway.
  • A natural on-ramp to Division B in middle school for students who want to keep going.

ExploraVision

A K–12 science innovation competition where student teams envision and describe a future technology that solves a real problem. A written and illustrated format — no building or construction required.

Highlights

  • Rewards conceptual and scientific thinking over engineering/construction skills.
  • Team-based, open to elementary-aged students.
  • A good complement for students who enjoy the ideas behind physics more than hands-on building.
  • National recognition for top entries.
